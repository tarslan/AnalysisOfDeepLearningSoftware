**Program:** Computer Science, University of Nebraska–Lincoln (UNL)
**Research:** Unified Multilayer Security Analysis Framework (UMSAF)
**Target graduation:** August 2027
**Prepared:** November 3, 2026

> **Verification note:** This document combines previously discussed UNL School of Computing requirements with planning recommendations. Confirm all requirements, deadlines, and forms with your advisor, supervisory committee, School of Computing, and UNL Graduate Studies. The timeline is proposed, not an official university schedule.

## 1. Understanding the milestones

1. **Academic eligibility:** Complete required doctoral coursework and other program prerequisites.
2. **Comprehensive examination:** Submit and defend a written dissertation proposal, demonstrating readiness for independent doctoral research.
3. **Admission to candidacy:** Complete the applicable Graduate Studies paperwork and requirements after passing the examination.

**Is two-thirds of the dissertation required?** No published School of Computing requirement identified in the previous review specified that two-thirds of the dissertation must be written before the comprehensive examination. The proposal should include a substantial literature review, a defined research gap, methodology, preliminary research, and a plan for the work remaining. Your committee may impose additional expectations.

## 4. Suggested UMSAF proposal outline

| Section | Recommended content |
|---|---|
| 1. Introduction | Security challenges in Python-based deep learning systems |
| 2. Problem Statement | Limitations of isolated security analysis approaches |
| 3. Literature Review | SCA, SBOM, native binary analysis, vulnerability detection, and cross-layer research |
| 4. Research Gap | Evidence showing what existing approaches do not adequately address |
| 5. Research Questions | Specific and measurable questions about UMSAF |
| 6. Proposed Framework | UMSAF architecture, components, and data flows |
| 7. Preliminary Research | Existing prototype, feasibility studies, or experimental findings |
| 8. Research Methodology | Design science and empirical evaluation |
| 9. Evaluation Plan | Baselines, datasets, metrics, and threats to validity |
| 10. Expected Contributions | Theoretical, methodological, and practical contributions |
| 11. Remaining Work | Experiments, dissertation writing, and completion schedule |
| 12. References | Verified scholarly references |

This outline is recommended rather than a prescribed UNL chapter format.
